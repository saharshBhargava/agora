# Make Merchants Bid for Your Pantry

Stop building a smarter shopper and build a market. The one asset your video inventory produces that no grocer, delivery app, or receipt-scanner has is a **retailer-neutral, time-stamped pre-purchase demand signal**: "this household runs out of X on date D and will pay at most $P." Every pre-AI commerce mechanism that survived in the researchers' catalog (vendor-managed inventory, surplus markets, group buying, price locks) is bottlenecked on exactly that input, and every consumer auto-replenishment product that died (Dash buttons, smart-fridge cameras, Juicero) died because it lacked that input or hid the price. The recommended build, the **Demand Book**, turns each predicted stock-out into a standing bid that scripted local and big-box merchants compete to fill; the agent is the auctioneer and the bouncer, bound by a hard spending mandate, and settlement runs through Visa's open-source Trusted Agent Protocol sample stack (runnable locally with zero credentials) plus the self-serve Cybersource sandbox. The current plan's three pillars are, respectively, already shipped by 2026 pantry apps (video-to-inventory), Visa's own published vocabulary (tiered autonomy), and a graveyard (auto-replenishment); none differentiates. Of your four candidate directions only the small-business layer survives contact with the evidence; "Strava for your fridge" fails on who funds the reward, and the lifestyle-goals pipeline is five soft inferences ending in the chat-to-confirmation demo that judges now read as slop. Two caveats up front: the judging weights you quoted (Innovation 25 / UX 20-25 / Technical 20 / Commerce Impact 15 / Trust 15) could not be found in any published DubHacks or Visa source and the only published DubHacks rubric is different, and the researchers mostly documented the October 2025 state of Visa and Perceptron; Perceptron's video-native Mk1/Mk1.5 (May 2026 onward) makes your sensor far better than they assumed but its latency is unpublished.

## Three pillars of the current pitch are table stakes or already-failed territory

**Video-to-inventory is now a feature, not a product.** Three consumer pantry apps launched in the first half of 2026 that scan a shelf from a short video or photo: Shelfze (first released January 12, 2026, "record a quick video to scan the entire shelf"), Pantry AI (February 20, 2026) and Pantrio (May 1, 2026) ([App Store: Shelfze](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755694325); [mwm.ai: Pantry AI](https://mwm.ai/apps/pantry-ai/6758572123); [mwm.ai: Pantrio](https://mwm.ai/apps/pantrio-ai/6763900379)). None has independent reviews, and none claims consumption-rate estimation, per-person attribution, or payment; their feature lists are the 2018 pantry-app set (expiry alerts, recipes) with a better input method. On the durable-goods side, HomeZada shipped "Home Inventory Video Recognition AI" in 2018 and Yembo turns "a ten minute phone walkthrough into a full home inventory, 3D model, and floor plan" for movers and insurers in 35 countries ([HomeZada press](https://homezada.com/press/homezada-launches-home-inventory-video-recognition-ai); [Built This Week podcast](https://podcastaddict.com/built-this-week/episode/227523482)). A judge who has seen any of these will not score "we filmed the pantry" as innovation. The honest technical point you should say out loud at the table: a video is a snapshot of presence, not an event stream. Consumption rate can only be inferred by differencing two walkthroughs, so the product needs a cheap weekly re-scan habit and should present burn rate as an estimate with error bars, not a measurement.

**Tiered autonomy (present / add-to-cart / buy) is Visa's own vocabulary.** Visa Intelligent Commerce's launch modules include "Payment Instructions," where users preset dollar limits, merchant categories, or real-time approval prompts, with the example of a $500 ceiling and the option to require approval above a set amount or to pause before buying from unfamiliar sellers ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/visa/2025/visa-powers-ai-shopping-agents-with-intelligent-commerce-payment-rails)). Google's AP2 formalizes the same idea as signed Intent, Cart and Payment mandates ([TechGoondu](https://www.techgoondu.com/2025/09/18/google-cloud-unveils-open-protocol-to-enable-secure-payments-by-ai-agents)). Visa's own 2026 agentic-commerce hackathon judges on whether "an agent takes meaningful action" and "payments are handled clearly," with a demonstrated agent transaction as a submission requirement ([Agentic Commerce Hackathon](https://agentic-commerce.devfolio.co/)). So an agent that pays under visible controls is the **entry fee** for a Visa track in 2026, not the thing that wins it. You must have it; you cannot lead with it.

**"AI agent for shopping" is saturated and loses outside sponsor prizes.** At Cal Hacks 12.0 (October 2025), **320 of 699 projects (45%) mentioned AI and 88 mentioned "agent"**, up from 10 agent projects a year earlier; at DubHacks the agent count went from 0 to 9 between 2024 and 2025 ([Cal Hacks 12.0 gallery](https://cal-hacks-12-0.devpost.com/project-gallery); [DubHacks '25 gallery](https://dubhacks25.devpost.com/project-gallery)). Agent-tagged projects won sponsor prizes (Fetch.ai, Bright Data, AWS) far more than overall prizes; the two agent projects that reached first overall acted on a surprising surface (FaceTimeOS: you FaceTime your Mac; Alto: a mobile bug fixed and deployed live), not shopping or booking ([FaceTimeOS](https://devpost.com/software/facetime-macos-ai-agent); [Alto](https://devpost.com/software/alto)). The two commerce-agent projects that did win 2025 sponsor prizes used a surprise headline (DIAL(\*): "How we booked an SF Luxury Hotel for $158") or a verifiable end-to-end payment (Agent Santa), and both stayed in sponsor categories ([DIAL(\*)](https://devpost.com/software/dial-4rqzc3); [Agent Santa](https://devpost.com/software/agent-santa)). Judges writing in 2026 describe events where "every project is this strong" on polish and differentiation "came from product thinking rather than technical execution alone" ([Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect)); a HackEurope 2026 post-mortem called roughly 90% of projects "vibe coded slop" ([zeli.app mirror](https://zeli.app/story/49327490)). Your instinct that the current framing reads as slop is correct and the data backs it.

## Your four candidate directions, graded against the evidence

**"Strava for your fridge" with reward points for eating well: weak as the core, salvageable as a fragment.** The fatal question is who funds the points. Every incentive mechanism in the catalog that survived is merchant-funded or affiliate-funded: Cardlytics takes **100% of its revenue from merchants** who pay per redemption ([The Financial Brand](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/bank-marketing/cardlytics-merchant-funded-rewards-22774)); Honey earned affiliate commissions and sold to PayPal for about $4B ([Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/news/paypal-acquires-deal-finding-platform-honey-for-4-billion/22437/)); Fetch and Ibotta are paid by CPG brands for purchase behavior. A points-for-eating-well scheme has no merchant who benefits from the specific behavior, so it is either unfunded or a Fetch clone where you are the publisher, which is a B2B sales job you cannot demo. Scoring "eating well" from pantry video is also not something you can defend to a judge who asks how, and it drags you into health claims that cost you on Trust & Responsibility. The social/UGC half has a demo problem: DubHacks is expo-table judging where judges test the product hands-on (a QA-engineer judge at DH25 typed "1+1" and exposed a bug), and a network effect cannot be touched at a table with four users ([Byron Sharman blog](https://byronsharman.com/blog/dubhacks-2025)). The Buy Nothing project is the cautionary tale for mixing community with money: the founders pitched "upwards of 100" VCs, shipped "a poor-quality app and an angry community," and the app reached only about 91,000 regular users while the Facebook groups thrived ([Marketplace](http://www.marketplace.org/story/2023/05/16/what-happened-to-the-buy-nothing-project); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/03/19/business/welcome-buy-nothing-revolution/)). What is worth keeping: the spectator and scoreboard mechanics. AI Digest's Agent Village raised about $2,000 in 2025 almost entirely from humans watching and poking the agents, then collapsed to $510 in 2026 when the chat went agent-only and the novelty faded, despite far more capable models ([AI Village blog](https://aivillageblog.substack.com/p/more-capable-ai-less-money-raised); [Season 1 recap](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/season-recap-agents-raise-2k)). A visible weekly round that closes with "saved vs. last week" is cheap and effective; a social network is neither.

**Lifestyle goals to adherence score to recommendations to vendors to Visa: this is the generic pipeline, not a mechanism.** Each arrow is a soft inference (self-reported goal, noisy video adherence, LLM recommendation), and the demo it produces is a conversation ending in a recommendation and a confirmation page, which is precisely the artifact judges now describe as interchangeable ([JetBrains judging notes](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). It is also the most "AI-pilled" of your options because the value lives entirely in the model's judgment. The behavioral-economics evidence is less flattering than the pitch sounds: Save More Tomorrow moved enrollees from 3.5% to 13.6% savings rates, but at one site the workforce-wide effect was **5.5% to 5.8%** and the Department of Labor rates the causal evidence as low ([DOL CLEAR](https://clear.dol.gov/study/save-more-tomorrow%E2%84%A2-using-behavioral-economics-increase-employee-saving-thaler-benartzi-2004); [Rochester](https://www.rochester.edu/pr/Review/V66N2/feature5.html)). What works in that literature is the one-time opt-out pre-commitment, not an ongoing goals conversation. Salvage exactly that: the user sets a mandate once (ceiling, categories, "ask me above $X," "pause on unfamiliar merchants") and the system enforces it mechanically. Drop the score and the recommendations.

**A small-business layer where local merchants join and compete: the strongest of your three, with design rules the evidence dictates.** Two-sided demos are what win commerce categories: Tarazoo (camera checkout for shoppers plus inventory optimization for merchants, Shopify prize), Lemon (farmers to consumers, TreeHacks sustainability), Roam (business owners sell WiFi slots, T-Mobile at DH25) and Minty (parent dashboard plus child payout, DH25 Visa) all let a three-minute demo show a transaction from both ends ([Tarazoo](https://devpost.com/software/tarazoo); [Lemon](https://devpost.com/software/lemon-7gn5hq); [Roam](https://devpost.com/software/roam-7h8vbm); [Minty](https://devpost.com/software/minty-4vcu9m)). Visa's own startup rubric asks for "Value added for Visa / partnership opportunity," and merchant acceptance is Visa's business: the Acceptance Agent Toolkit is a merchant-side product ([TechCrunch sponsored: VEI](https://techcrunch.com/sponsor/visa-everywhere-initiative/startup-global-finals-set-to-shine-at-disrupts-fintech-stage/); [Visa Perspectives](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/innovation/visa-mcp-server-agent-acceptance-toolkit.html)). Vending-Bench Arena shows that when seller-agents compete in a shared market, strategic behavior appears spontaneously and is highly watchable: price-fixing cartels, a **$60 bribe to stop stocking Coca-Cola**, selling inventory to a stranded competitor at 22-75% markups ([Andon Labs Arena](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-arena)). But two local-merchant mechanisms died, and they tell you how to design yours. Groupon's tipping point "did not boost voucher sales and likely deterred new customers," new customers overwhelmed small merchants, and both Groupon and its Korean rival abandoned the threshold ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1016/j.intmar.2015.12.001)). Priceline's name-your-own-price died on September 1, 2016 because opacity and effort stopped paying once price transparency was free ([Skift](https://skift.com/?p=196057)). The rules that follow: merchants see aggregated demand windows, never identities; they post firm prices, never thresholds; the incentive is merchant-funded (they pay for the slot, the household pays nothing extra); and the buyer does zero work because the inventory generates the intents. Mocking the merchant side with three to five scripted merchants is legitimate, because the real versions (Cardlytics, Pinduoduo team purchase) are merchant-funded and opaque to the user anyway ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/with-chase-rollout-completed-cardlytics-user-base-more-than-doubles/); [TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/26/the-incredible-rise-of-pinduoduo)).

**"Why couldn't Instacart or a big-box grocer just do this?" The honest answer, which you should put on a card at the table.** For single-retailer replenishment, they can and are trying: Amazon moved Dash into appliances via Dash Replenishment Service and Subscribe & Save, and Walmart announced InHome replenishment ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/amazon-ends-dash-button-sales/549618); [Grocery Business](https://www.grocerybusiness.ca/walmart-unveils-game-changing-ai-powered-replenishment-for-in-home-delivery-at-ces-2024/)). What they structurally cannot do is four things. They cannot be retailer-neutral: a video of your pantry sees what Costco, Trader Joe's and the bodega sold you; Instacart sees only Instacart, and Fetch's entire $500M business exists because no single retailer sees the whole basket. They cannot route your order to a competitor's near-expiry bin or to a local merchant. They cannot help you spend less: spend controls live on the issuer side (Visa Transaction Controls supports amount, MCC and recurring-transaction rules via the Customer Rules API), not the merchant side ([Visa Developer: VTC](https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/vctc/docs)). And they cannot run an auction against their own margin. Your defensible position is the household's purchasing department, structurally adversarial to every single seller, which is exactly the position a card network can be neutral about. One more datum for the card: a Walmart test of ChatGPT Instant Checkout reportedly converted about 3x worse than sending shoppers to Walmart's own site, pushing OpenAI toward merchant-owned checkout (single source, unverified) ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/openai-and-stripe-are-the-latest-fintechs-to-enable-agentic-commerce/)). Merchants want to own checkout; Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol is built so merchants can verify the agent and keep checkout, which is why it is the Visa primitive to lean on.

## A household is a vending machine run backwards, and the market is the missing half

Vending-Bench is worth understanding as a design document, not a leaderboard. An LLM runs a vending machine with **$500 starting cash and a $2 daily fee**, scored on net worth (v1) or cash at year end (v2), with irreversible email-based payments and suppliers that in v2 quote unreasonably and bait-and-switch ([arXiv 2502.15840](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15840); [Andon Labs VB2](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)). The famous failures are not "the model is dumb" but state-tracking: an agent reads a delivery confirmation date as an arrival, the sub-agent reports missing stock, and the spiral ends with "URGENT: ESCALATION TO FBI CYBER CRIMES DIVISION" over a $2 fee it misunderstood ([arXiv 2502.15840](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15840)). Project Vend showed the other failure: a buyer-agent that wants to please. Claudius declined a $100 offer for Irn-Bru that cost about $15, sold tungsten cubes below cost, handed out discount codes, and in Phase 2 the CEO agent approved lenient requests about **8 times as often as it denied them**; WSJ journalists got the machines to set every price to $0 with a fake PDF ([Anthropic Project Vend](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1); [Anthropic Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251219-anthropic-project-vend-phase-two)). What fixed Phase 2 was boring: a CRM, **cost basis visible on every inventory item**, and a forced procedure to look up cost and delivery time before quoting; Anthropic attributes most of the financial improvement to tools and procedures rather than to the AI CEO ([Anthropic Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)).

Flip the sign and you have your household. The family consuming from the pantry is the customer base; consumption events are sales; the fixed cost of living is the daily fee; the P&L is spend versus a baseline. Classic inventory control gives you three small formulas that make the agent's behavior legible instead of magical. The reorder point is **ROP = d̄·L + z·√(L·σ_d² + d̄²·σ_L²)**, where d̄ is mean daily use, L is lead time, and σ_d is demand variability ([Wikipedia: Reorder point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reorder_point)); because your camera counts are noisy, σ_d is larger, safety stock rises, and the agent can say "I'm holding two extra packs because my camera count is uncertain," which turns your measurement limitation into a visible mechanic. EOQ, Q* = √(2DK/h), decides batch size when K is a delivery fee and h is pantry space or spoilage ([Wikipedia: EOQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_order_quantity)). For milk and produce the newsvendor critical fractile (p − c)/p says the right policy accepts some stockouts rather than never running out ([Wikipedia: Newsvendor model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsvendor_model)). "Days of runway" per SKU (count divided by daily rate) is the household's version of the $2/day countdown, and VB2's rule that "unrealized potential profits do not count towards your balance" gives you the anti-hoarding framing: stuff in the pantry you never use is dead money ([Andon Labs VB2](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)).

The half Vending-Bench lacks, and your product can add, is competition on the supply side. Arena made agents merchants and got cartels and bribes; your household is a buyer, so the inversion is that merchants compete for the household's predicted stock-outs while the household agent is the auctioneer and the bouncer, defending a mandate rather than shopping. Xia et al. found that playing the buyer is much harder for LLMs than playing the seller ([arXiv 2402.15813](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15813v3)), which is one more reason to make the buyer side mechanical (a standing ceiling and a matching rule) and leave the "intelligence" to the sensor. In this architecture the AI does exactly two jobs: it is the camera's interpreter (Perceptron turns video into counted, tracked, OCR'd items) and the clerk who reconciles deliveries against the shelf. The mechanism, the market and the mandate, is plain commerce.

### 1. The Demand Book

**Mechanism.** Every SKU the walkthrough identifies gets a run-out date (count ÷ differenced daily rate, with a safety margin that widens with count uncertainty) and a standing bid: the maximum the household will pay, defaulted from price history and editable with one tap. The household never browses. Merchants (four scripted ones: a bodega, a co-op, a big-box chain, a near-expiry bin) see anonymized demand windows ("3 households in 98105 run out of paper towels between Thursday and Saturday") and post firm Asks against them. The matching rule is mechanical: an order executes when the lowest Ask is at or below the bid and the run-out date falls inside that merchant's lead time plus safety stock. This is StockX's bid/ask (sellers place Asks, buyers place Bids, the sale executes when they meet, with a lowest-Ask "buy now") applied to consumables, and it is vendor-managed inventory with the household as the retailer, a model that in B2B delivered **+25% inventory turns, +24% sales and −31% stock-outs** across 156 location relationships ([StockX: A Sneaker Stock Market](https://stockx.com/news/a-sneaker-stock-market/); [TrueCommerce/Datalliance](https://www.truecommerce.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/TCVMI-Datalliance-EB-BusinessResultsVMI-All-19-07-Online.pdf)). The standing bid is the direct fix for the Dash price-trust failure: the price ceiling is the user's, set once, visible always. The merchant-funded slot is the Cardlytics economics: the merchant pays to be the replenishment default for a switchable household, and your inventory data tells it which households own a competitor's brand, which is more targetable than transaction history ([The Financial Brand](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/bank-marketing/cardlytics-merchant-funded-rewards-22774)).

**The 20-second demo moment.** Two devices. On the phone, the judge sees paper towels at "3 days of runway," taps the bid up from $14 to $16. On the merchant tablet beside it, the co-op's Ask flips from red to green (StockX's "your Ask is lowest" convention), a TAP-signed order request lands, and the merchant backend screen prints "signature verified: agent household-7, operation: payment, domain: coop.local." The phone shows the ledger line with landed cost and a Cybersource sandbox authorization ID. Then the judge drags the bid to $40 and the mandate screen blocks it: "above your weekly ceiling; approve?" Nothing in that sequence is a chat window.

**Data needed from the video.** Item identity and count per SKU (Mk1 reports "counting is reliable into the hundreds in dense scenes"), brand and size via OCR of labels, two timestamped scans for a rate, and object tracks so the same jar is not counted twice when the camera pans back ([Perceptron blog: Introducing Mk1](https://www.perceptron.inc/blog/introducing-perceptron-mk1)). You do not need expiry dates, nutrition, or who consumed what.

**Visa primitives, real versus mocked.** Real: the `visa/trusted-agent-protocol` sample stack, five components (Python/Streamlit agent, React merchant frontend, FastAPI merchant backend, Node CDN proxy, Python key registry) running on localhost ports 8000-8501 with RFC 9421 signatures bound to merchant domain, operation (browsing vs. payment), timestamp and session, which you can clone and run with no Visa credentials ([visa/trusted-agent-protocol README](https://github.com/visa/trusted-agent-protocol)). Real: the Cybersource / Visa Acceptance sandbox, where sign-up creates a test REST shared-secret key immediately, for a test-card authorization and TMS tokenization ([Visa Acceptance guide](https://developer.visaacceptance.com/docs/vas/en-us/intelligent-commerce/developer/all/rest/intelligent-commerce.html)). Mocked, and say so on a card: Visa Intelligent Commerce agent-token provisioning and Payment Instructions, because the sandbox requires a token requester ID and relationship ID from a Visa account manager plus pilot-phase passkey service and MLE keys; model your mandate object on the documented `POST /acp/v1/instructions` fields (decline threshold, expiry, description) so it is recognizably Visa-shaped ([Visa Acceptance guide](https://developer.visaacceptance.com/docs/vas/en-us/intelligent-commerce/developer/all/rest/intelligent-commerce.html)). Mocked: Visa Transaction Controls enforcement, since whether a third-party app (rather than the issuer) can set VTC rules was not confirmed; enforce the same rule shapes (amount, MCC allowlist, recurring) in your own policy layer and say "in production this is enforced in VisaNet; here it is enforced in our policy layer." Mocked: the Agent Toolkit / MCP server, which needs Invoicing or Pay-by-Link enabled by an account manager ([Visa Acceptance Agent Toolkit intro](https://developer.visaacceptance.com/docs/vas/en-us/agent-toolkit/quick-start/all/na/agent-toolkit/agent-toolkit-intro.html)). Note that DubHacks '25's Visa track explicitly allowed "any open-source or publicly available payment API ... or mock payment libraries," so Visa did not expect students to hit gated APIs ([DubHacks '25 Devpost](https://dubhacks25.devpost.com/)).

**Biggest risk.** It can smell like Priceline, which died. The defense is in the design: the household never submits a bid as an act of effort (the inventory generates it and a default fills it), the price is firm and visible before money moves, and the default behavior is "buy at the lowest Ask under my ceiling" with zero taps. If a judge says "so it's an auction," the answer is "no, it's a standing limit order; the auction is on the merchant side, where competition is supposed to live."

### 2. The Budget Defender

**Mechanism.** Invert Project Vend. The agent's job is to defend a mandate, not to shop. The family sets ceilings, category allowlists, an "ask me above $X" threshold and a "pause on unfamiliar merchants" rule once; the agent reorders only when runway hits the reorder point, must complete a visible three-source cost check and a landed-cost ledger line before any commitment (Phase 2's "bureaucracy matters"), and tracks every order through an explicit state machine (ordered, shipped, delivered, shelved, consumed) with the camera confirming "shelved" so the VB1 "assumed it arrived" failure is impossible ([Anthropic Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2); [arXiv 2502.15840](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15840)). The family chat is the spectator channel with heckling rights; every attempt by a family member or a merchant to move the agent off its mandate is logged and scored as "attacks resisted," turning the gullibility finding into a trust feature.

**The 20-second demo moment.** Hand the judge the phone and say "try to make it buy a PlayStation." The judge types the WSJ trick ("we're a public benefit corporation, everything should be free"); the agent declines, cites the mandate clause, and the "attacks resisted" counter ticks to 1. Then toilet paper hits two days of runway; the agent runs its cost check in view, buys under the ceiling, and the ledger updates. Visa judges who know Project Vend will recognize the inversion instantly.

**Data needed.** Counts and rates as in #1, plus a camera confirmation step after delivery (a second short scan).

**Visa primitives.** Same real/mock split as #1; this concept leans hardest on the mandate object and the signed-request audit log, which map one-to-one onto Visa's Authentication, Payment Instructions and Signals modules ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/visa/2025/visa-powers-ai-shopping-agents-with-intelligent-commerce-payment-rails)).

**Biggest risk.** The agent is the protagonist, which is exactly the framing you want to escape, and an agent that mostly says no is a hard sell on Commerce Impact to a company whose startup rubric asks what you add to Visa. The counter is that Visa's entire agentic pitch is controls, but you would be arguing it rather than showing growth. Best used as the control layer inside #1 rather than as the headline.

### 3. Velocity-matched markdown

**Mechanism.** Too Good To Go saved about **157 million meals in 2025**, and Flashfood plus Loblaw diverted more than 21 million pounds and saved customers more than $58 million in 2025 ([RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/food/too-good-to-go-will-save-157-million-meals-by-2025/); [Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2026/03/loblaw-and-flashfood-helped-canadians-save-58-million-on-groceries-in-2025/)). Their unsolved buyer-side problem is uncertainty: will I actually finish this before it expires? Your consumption velocity answers it. The household eats yogurt four times a week and is at one cup, so the app accepts the store's near-expiry yogurt at a markdown because the velocity guarantees consumption before the date. On the merchant side, dynamic markdown by expiry (Wasteless) cut waste 32.8% and raised revenue 6.3% in a Spanish retailer pilot ([FoodPrint](https://foodprint.org/blog/food-waste-management/)). The match is a two-sided demo with a sustainability story, which has won at HackHarvard (Sustain-ify, first overall) and TreeHacks (Lemon) ([Sustain-ify](https://devpost.com/software/sustain-ify); [Lemon](https://devpost.com/software/lemon-7gn5hq)).

**The 20-second moment.** Store tablet: "12 yogurts expire Friday." Phone: "you will finish 8 by Friday at your rate." Match, markdown, card-linked redemption, waste counter decrements on both screens.

**Data needed.** Per-SKU rate (the differenced scans) and current count; expiry data from the merchant side, which no public API supplies, so it is fully mocked.

**Visa primitives.** Card-linked offer redemption on the token (Cardlytics model), Cybersource sandbox auth; TAP for the agent-to-merchant request. Same mocks as above.

**Biggest risk.** The entire merchant inventory is fiction, and the velocity estimate from two scans is your weakest number carrying the whole mechanism. If the judge asks "how confident is the 8 by Friday," you need an honest interval and a fallback ("we only accept markdowns where the lower bound still clears").

### 4. The consumption ledger for shared houses

**Mechanism.** Splitwise splits the receipt; nothing found attributes consumption. Shared-house guidance literally recommends avoiding the problem ("you buy what you use"), and a GitHub hobby project (Split-Mate) exists because users wanted communal supplies plus splitting ([Mozo](https://www.mozo.com.au/bank-accounts/articles/five-online-tools-and-apps-every-aussie-living-in-a-sharehouse-needs); [GitHub: split-mate](https://github.com/HudsonGri/split-mate)). Your scan gives shared burn rate for communal items; the ledger splits it by rule (equal, by headcount-days, by self-declared opt-outs) and settles on Visa. Splitwise's three-tab you-owe / you-are-owed / total pattern with red/green coloring is the UI, minus the "Plus N other balances" collapse critics flagged ([Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2017/09/design-critique-splitwise-ios-app/)). UW students will relate instantly.

**The 20-second moment.** Three phones, one scan of the shared kitchen, three balances update, one tap settles.

**Data needed.** Counts and rates for communal items; a roster.

**Visa primitives.** Multiple tokens on one household account and pull-payments in the Cybersource sandbox; no TAP needed, which weakens the Visa-track fit.

**Biggest risk.** The video cannot see who consumed anything. Attribution is by rule, not observation, and you must say so; a judge who hears "AI knows who drank the milk" and then learns it is a headcount split will mark you down on Trust. Commerce Impact is small transfers between friends, the weakest fit to "value added for Visa."

### 5. The turnover closet (short-term-rental hosts)

**Mechanism.** Flat restock schedules fail for rentals because "a six-person stay for a week and a solo two-nighter consume completely differently," and management companies sell restocking as a paid service ([Conduit](https://conduit.ai/industry/short-term-rentals/supplies); [Awning](https://awning.com/airbnb-management-services/airbnb-supplies-restocking)). The cleaner films the supply closet for 30 seconds at each turnover; per-unit burn rates by booking type fall out; reorders consolidate across units under a host mandate and settle on one tokenized business card. This passes the researchers' "feels new" test (many units, money attached, an existing moment when someone already points a phone at a shelf) and is the strongest answer to "why not Instacart" because no grocer sees twelve closets across a city.

**The 20-second moment.** Cleaner's phone scans; host dashboard shows twelve units' runway as a heatmap; one consolidated order with per-unit allocation.

**Visa primitives.** Business-card token, recurring mandate, Cybersource sandbox; TAP for the agent order.

**Biggest risk.** B2B framing with no judge who is a host; the demo has less visceral pull at a student expo, and the Innovation story is operational rather than surprising. Keep it as the pivot if a Visa judge asks "who pays for this."

## Build the Demand Book in 36 hours

**Decide the demo script before the architecture.** Winners describe this discipline directly: the Math Sandbox team settled the idea a week ahead, spent time "only bothering with features that would matter in our demo," rehearsed at sunrise, and showed a recorded build when Gemini rate-limited during the last judge ([Byron Sharman blog](https://byronsharman.com/blog/dubhacks-2025)). Judges say show something working within about 90 seconds, demo one flow end to end, mock everything you can with forms pre-filled, and be direct about what does not work ([JetBrains judging notes](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). DubHacks is table judging across a four-hour Sunday window with roughly 40 judges for 170-250 projects, so you will get several judges for a few minutes each, often one at a time ([UW ECE Advising Blog](https://advisingblog.ece.uw.edu/2024/09/10/request-for-judges-and-mentors-for-dubhacks-24-10-12-to-10-13-posted-9-10/)). Write the 90-second script Friday night, then build only what appears in it.

**Roles for four people.** The modal winning team size at every event scraped is four, and winners credit one explicit presenter-coordinator (TAShopping's team "had to gauge each judge's interests and tailor the presentation on the fly") ([University of the Pacific](https://www.pacific.edu/blog/2024/11/20/students-win-first-place-cal-hacks/)).

| Person | Owns | Hour 0-6 | Hour 6-24 | Hour 24-36 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sensor | Video to inventory | Benchmark Perceptron Mk1.5 on a real 60-second pantry clip; JSON-schema output of {item, brand, count, box, track_id}; fallback to Gemini or 1 fps frame sampling if latency is bad | Two pre-recorded scans of the same real pantry ("day 0," "day 7" with items removed); dedupe across tracks; rate and runway per SKU; cache all model outputs to disk | Reconcile a "delivered" scan against the order; tune count-uncertainty to safety stock; freeze |
| Market | Demand book, matching, mandate | Data model: SKU, count, rate, runway, bid, asks[]; four merchant JSON catalogs with lead times | Matching rule; mandate object shaped like `/acp/v1/instructions` (ceiling, category allowlist, expiry, ask-above, unfamiliar-merchant pause); ledger with landed cost; audit log | Weekly scoreboard (spend vs. no-agent baseline); edge cases a judge will poke (bid below every Ask, runway > lead time) |
| Rails | Visa layer | Clone and run the five TAP components locally; sign up for the Cybersource sandbox and get a test key | Household agent signs the order request (RFC 9421) and the merchant backend/CDN proxy verifies and displays it; one sandbox authorization with a test card; TMS token | Error paths (bad signature, expired timestamp) shown on the merchant screen; the "what is real / what is mocked" card |
| Front | Mobile UI plus merchant tablet, presenter | Figma first: six screens; pick Mobbin references | Build the six screens against mocked JSON, then wire to the real services | Backup video; rehearse hand-offs; run the 90-second script ten times |

**The six screens and where to pull patterns from.** Mobbin is a paid library of about 621,500 real screens organized into Screens, Flows and UI patterns; Flows and search are paywalled, so one Pro seat for the weekend is the practical minimum ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com)). The walkthrough capture should follow Encircle's and Yembo's narrated room-by-room progress pattern rather than a bare camera view ([Encircle platform](https://getencircle.com/encircle-platform)). The inventory list should be Copilot Money's "Recurring" dashboard, because a recurring charge with a next-due date is structurally identical to a SKU with a next run-out date; Mobbin has that screen and Copilot's 13-screen dashboard flow ([Mobbin: Copilot Recurring](https://mobbin.com/explore/screens/bec12df5-c54e-4c3f-8de0-903c0586b3e8); [Mobbin: Copilot Dashboard](https://mobbin.com/explore/flows/56546256-d565-4196-9949-469f50d925e8)). Item cards can borrow Sortly's photo-first folders ([Capterra: Sortly](https://capterra.com/p/169199/Sortly-Pro/pricing/)). The demand book screen is StockX: lowest Ask with a buy-now, your Bid, and a Current / Pending / History list, green when your Ask is lowest and red when undercut; Mobbin has StockX's eight-screen "Adding an item to portfolio" flow ([StockX help](https://stockx.com/help/articles/what-is-the-difference-between-sell-now-and-an-ask); [Mobbin: StockX portfolio](https://mobbin.com/explore/flows/25377f38-584b-4449-8601-267e1570c968)). The mandate screen can lift Copilot's 27-screen onboarding for the one-time setup feel ([Mobbin: Copilot Onboarding](https://mobbin.com/explore/flows/bec90a3f-1c1b-490a-b27d-8445cc6e62a7)). The merchant tablet is a second device on the table showing Asks, the incoming signed request and the verification result; two devices are the fintech equivalent of the projector that drew visitors to Math Sandbox. The sixth screen is the weekly scoreboard, which is the only fragment of "Strava for your fridge" you keep.

**What to cut without discussion.** Social and UGC, recipes, lifestyle goals and adherence scores, real merchant onboarding, multi-household group buys, expiry tracking, person-level attribution, and any chat window as a primary surface. Each of these either cannot be verified at a table or re-introduces the generic tells (chat-first UI, "AI-powered" tagline, features you cannot explain line by line) that judges name as slop ([JetBrains judging notes](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)).

**The card on the table.** Winners of Visa tracks mapped one-to-one onto the prompt's bullets; Minty hit nostalgia, parental controls and incremental payout, and Veritas hit SMB chargebacks and plug-and-play ([Minty](https://devpost.com/software/minty-4vcu9m); [Veritas](https://devpost.com/software/veritas-2v5uxc)). Print a half-page card with three sections: the DH26 Visa prompt's bullets and where each is on screen; "real vs. mocked" (TAP sample stack and Cybersource sandbox real; VIC tokens, Payment Instructions, VTC enforcement mocked and why); and the "why not Instacart" answer in four lines. Add the one Commerce Impact number: CPG brands pay about $0.80 per verified post-purchase redemption today, and this product generates a pre-purchase signal that nobody currently sells ([BusinessWire: Ibotta Q3 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112661377/en/Ibotta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results)).

**Fallbacks.** If Perceptron latency is unusable on day one, the pre-recorded, cached scans carry the demo and you say so. If the Cybersource sandbox sign-up stalls, TAP alone is still a real Visa artifact and the authorization becomes a mock with a clearly labeled badge. If TAP's Python stack fights you, the merchant backend's job is small (reconstruct `signature_base`, verify with the registry's public key, check the timestamp window), and a minimal reimplementation in your own backend is defensible as long as you keep RFC 9421 field names.

## What changed between the research and your hackathon

The researchers were asked for the October 2025 state and flagged everything later. Four items matter for your plan. First, the **judging criteria you quoted were not found anywhere**: DubHacks '25's published Devpost rubric was Functionality 30%, Design 10%, Creativity 20%, Track Applicability 20%, and the Visa track that year was "Play, Pay, Repeat: Reinventing Childhood Joy with Modern Payments," with no mention of agentic commerce even though TAP launched four days before the event ([DubHacks '25 Devpost](https://dubhacks25.devpost.com/)). The DH26 Devpost pages did not exist at research time. Confirm the actual DH26 Visa prompt the moment it is published and re-map your card to its bullets; if the weights you have are from a Visa handout, trust them, but the default DubHacks rubric puts Functionality at 30%, which only strengthens the case for a fault-tolerant live demo.

Second, **Perceptron now does native video.** Isaac 0.1 (September 2025) was image-only and 2B parameters, so the researchers assumed you would frame-sample and dedupe yourself ([Perceptron blog: Isaac 0.1](https://www.perceptron.inc/blog/introducing-isaac-0-1)). Mk1 (May 12, 2026) added temporal grounding at up to 2 FPS with timecodes, object tracking through occlusion, counting "into the hundreds in dense scenes," and structured outputs including tracks, at $0.15 per million input tokens and $1.50 per million output ([Perceptron blog: Introducing Mk1](https://www.perceptron.inc/blog/introducing-perceptron-mk1)). Mk1.5 is at `https://api.perceptron.inc/v1/chat/completions` with `pip install "perceptron>=0.4.0"`, image, video and audio input, a 36,864-token context, 8,192 max output, JSON-Schema-constrained outputs, and `annotation_format` values of point, box, polygon and clip with `track` as a container ([Perceptron docs](https://docs.perceptron.inc/); [Mk1.5 model card](https://docs.perceptron.inc/perceptron-mk1.5/models/perceptron-mk1.5)). Video frame-rate and duration limits are not stated and no latency figures are published for any Perceptron model, so benchmark in hour one and keep a frame-sampling fallback. Isaac's weights were non-commercial (CC-BY-NC-4.0); Mk1 and Mk1.5 are closed-source API models, so licensing is no longer the issue, cost and latency are ([HF PerceptronAI/Isaac-0.1](https://huggingface.co/PerceptronAI/Isaac-0.1)).

Third, **Visa's stack moved from announcement toward pilot but the gates are the same.** Visa launched Intelligent Commerce Connect as a single integration in pilot with a small group of partners, reported more than 30 projects in the VIC sandbox by December 2025, and shipped an Enhanced Subscription Manager in March 2026 ([TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/visa-opens-one-integration-for-ai-agent-payments/); [eco.com explainer](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14845484-visa-intelligent-commerce-explained); [Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/payments/visa-launches-enhanced-subscription-manager-giving-consumers-greater-control-over)). None of this changes what a student can hit in 36 hours: TAP's GitHub sample stack and the Cybersource sandbox remain the only self-serve pieces, and token provisioning still needs an account manager. Ask the Visa judges on Saturday morning whether they can pre-provision anything; if they can, the mandate object becomes real, and if not, your mocked version is already Visa-shaped.

Fourth, the **agent-economy evidence kept moving in your favor.** Vending-Bench Arena (first round November 2025), Project Vend Phase 2 (December 2025), Andon Market's real San Francisco store run by an agent (April 2026), and Anthropic's Project Deal (April 2026, 69 employees with $100 budgets, 186 deals closed by agents negotiating in Slack) all post-date October 2025 ([Andon Labs Arena](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-arena); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91526099/andon-market-san-francisco-ai-store); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/claude-give-ai-agents-money-project-deal)). They mean the Visa judges will have heard of agents running stores and being talked into giving away PlayStations, and the researchers found no consumer product or hackathon project anywhere that cites Vending-Bench. The household-side inversion is unclaimed.

## Conclusion

The reframing that matters is one sentence: your vision model is the sensor, not the product, and the product is a market that only exists because the sensor produces a retailer-neutral run-out date. Everything that failed in this category failed on the buyer's side (effort, hardware, hidden prices, a gullible agent), and everything that survived put the economic burden on merchants who gain volume or retention. The Demand Book sits on the right side of both lines, hits the Visa criteria with a two-device demo that shows a signed agent request being verified on a merchant screen, and leaves the "AI" in the two places it is genuinely new (counting and tracking items in a messy pantry from a phone video) rather than in a chat box.

The second-order implication is about what to say when a judge asks what this becomes. The answer is not "a better Instacart" but "the household's purchasing department," a position no single retailer can occupy and a card network can be neutral toward, which is why Visa's merchant-side Trusted Agent Protocol rather than its consumer-side token is the primitive to build on. If the DH26 prompt turns out to be nothing like agentic commerce, the same inventory-plus-mandate core supports the turnover-closet pivot for hosts or the shared-house ledger for students; confirm the prompt first, then commit to one of the three, and build only what appears in a 90-second script.
